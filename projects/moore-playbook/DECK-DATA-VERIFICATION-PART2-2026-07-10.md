## 1. URGENT — the InnerScope royalty/spin-off model has no legitimate precedent, and its closest analogue drew SEC enforcement

This is the single most important finding across both research passes today. The deck's
InnerScope tranche models a 25%-of-gross-profit royalty plus "spin-off platform fees" (charging
newly-acquired/spun-off entities for their own PCAOB audit, Form 10, transfer agent, and IR
setup) as a recurring, SaaS-like revenue line reaching 70.5% Year 1 EBITDA margin, rising to
94.6% by Year 5. Research found:

- **No legitimate public company uses this exact structure.** The closest analogues — the Ault
  Alliance/BitNile/DPW Holdings lineage of OTC roll-up holding companies — are associated with an
  **actual SEC enforcement action** (Release 33-11222, August 2023) for misleading disclosures
  about related-party fees flowing between the parent and a subsidiary. The company paid a
  $700,000 penalty. **The nearest real-world comparison to this proposed model is a company the
  SEC charged for exactly this kind of related-party fee opacity.**
- **25% of gross profit is structurally much heavier than real franchise/licensing precedent.**
  Real franchise royalties run 4-8% of *revenue* (not gross profit); IP licensing runs 2-10% of
  *net sales*. Because gross profit is a smaller base than revenue, 25% of gross profit is
  typically equivalent to 10%+ of total revenue once margins are applied — well above normal
  franchise/licensing rates, before the subsidiary has paid any of its own operating costs.
- **"Spin-off platform fees" is not a recognized revenue model anywhere.** Real spin-offs (DTE
  Energy/DT Midstream, Jefferies/Vitesse) treat audit/legal/transfer-agent costs as one-time
  transaction costs to be *absorbed* — explicitly not recouped if the deal falls through. Charging
  a captive subsidiary for its own compliance setup and calling it a product is cost recovery
  relabeled as revenue, not a comparable business line.
- **The 70.5%→94.6% EBITDA margin trajectory has no comparable precedent.** No real asset-light
  holding company or royalty-income filer sustains margins in this range once real corporate
  overhead, audit, and IR costs are included.

**This is not a "cite it better" problem.** Recommend Matt and Capital/IR treat this as a real
open question about the InnerScope tranche's revenue model itself, with counsel's direct input,
before any version of this specific structure goes to an underwriter.

## 2. Pitch-deck pattern research — what actually works, and one caveat that applies directly here

- **Real deck teardowns** (TechCrunch's Pitch Deck Teardown series, Whitepage Studio's healthcare
  deck analyses) show credibility signals are stage-matched: payer/partner-logo walls at seed,
  cohort benchmarks at pre-seed, scale/EBITDA proof only once a company has actually reached
  scale. A recurring failure pattern: raising repeatedly (one cited example raised $30M across 8
  rounds) without hitting the scale the raises implied — investors specifically probe this.
- **Investor red flags** cited consistently: vague regulatory pathways, reimbursement plans that
  don't specify who pays and how, hockey-stick revenue with no stated churn/CAC assumptions, thin
  clinical validation (one slide out of thirty), and vanity top-line metrics without engagement
  data proving the product is actually used.
- **Flywheel/cross-sell narratives get proven with ONE hard, auditable metric tied to a real early
  cohort — not an aspirational claim.** Hims & Hers quantifies its own cross-sell flywheel as a
  specific 24-month LTV increase ($312→$441) attributable to bundling. Practo cites contribution-
  margin progression and a 98% B2B retention rate. GoRocky cites 30,000 patients and 60%+ repeat
  engagement before claiming platform-wide expansion. **Recommend finding or building one
  equivalent hard number for the OTCHealth cross-sell flywheel claim** rather than asserting it.
- **"Dormant asset, fund the reactivation" is a real, legitimate, well-precedented pitch** — this
  is good news for the core OTCHealth framing. The "buy, fix, hold forever" model (Bending Spoons,
  Constellation Software) and Retail Ecommerce Ventures' explicit reactivation-of-dormant-retail-
  brands thesis (Pier 1, Dressbarn, Linens 'n Things) are real, disclosed precedents for exactly
  this kind of pitch. The credible framing separates the *brand/customer relationship* (which
  persisted) from the *broken operating model* (being replaced) — this maps directly onto how
  the underwriting package already frames OTCHealth's own story.
- **The one caveat that applies directly to this deck:** public-market investors are increasingly
  skeptical of "shell-adjacent" holding structures, and the SEC has broadened its shell-company
  scrutiny for reverse-merger-style vehicles. **The deck should proactively distinguish OTCHealth/
  InnerScope from a pure listing-vehicle play by leading with operating substance** (the real
  customer base, the real product history) — which the underwriting package already does well —
  **rather than the corporate/spin-off structure**, which is exactly where §1's finding lives.

## 3. AWARE — fundraising-grade benchmarks
- **No hearing-rehab-specific company has disclosed real unit economics.** The most relevant real
  comp, Pear Therapeutics (a prescription digital therapeutics company), went through Chapter 11
  in 2023 — but its failure was reimbursement/payer-dependency, not clinical efficacy. **This
  actually supports AWARE's own DTC cash-pay model as the safer structure**, worth stating
  explicitly in the deck rather than avoiding the comparison.
- **Defensible LTV:CAC range: 3:1 to 5:1** — inferred from general digital-therapeutics margin
  data and consumer health-app benchmarks, explicitly not an observed hearing-rehab figure.
- **Defensible monthly churn range: 3-8%** — general health-app proxy, no hearing-specific data
  exists.
- **B2B2C clinic-channel anchor:** real audiology practice-management SaaS already sells to
  clinics at $100-500/month (amplifyOMS, Auditdata, HearLink, Sycle) — a legitimate anchor for
  what a clinic might pay to license AWARE, better than an invented figure.

## 4. InnerEase — fundraising-grade benchmarks
- **Oto Health (closest real comp) disclosed ~$5.1M in funding** (pre-seed + seed + a grant) but
  no valuation, LTV, CAC, or churn. A third-party estimate of "$4.9M ARR" conflicts with the
  disclosed funding history and should be treated as unverified, not cited.
- **Tinnitus TAM: ~25-27M US adults** experience tinnitus (NIDCD/NIH), with roughly 10% having
  chronic tinnitus (3+ months) — a real, citable market-size anchor.
- **Defensible wellness-app proxy range:** 3-8% freemium conversion, $50-70 first-year LTV,
  $5-15 CAC, 40-65% annual churn — explicitly a cross-category proxy (meditation/wellness apps),
  since no tinnitus-specific unit economics are publicly disclosed anywhere.

## 5. OTCHealth Companion — fundraising-grade benchmarks
- **None of the four comps used in the earlier research pass (SilverShield, SeniorSafe,
  SeniorTalk, Luna) have any disclosed funding, valuation, or revenue.** Two of the four have
  name-collision issues with unrelated products. This weakens their use as market-validation
  comps in a deck.
- **Better, real funded comps to cite instead: Savi Security ($7M seed, Acrew Capital, July
  2026) and Charm Security ($8M seed, March 2025).**
- **AgeTech is a real, growing category** — VC funding roughly $1.0-1.14B in 2025 YTD per one
  tracker, with real named rounds (Sensi.AI $45M Series C, ElliQ, Charlie, Carefull, CarePredict)
  worth citing for category momentum.
- **A real cautionary comp exists and is worth knowing:** Papa Inc. raised $150M at a $1.4B
  valuation, then had ~75% layoffs in 2024 amid Medicare Advantage billing scrutiny — worth
  understanding as a risk pattern in this category (revenue tied to a single payer channel).
- **The buyer-vs-user dynamic is real and sourced:** 69% of medical alert device purchases are
  made by someone other than the user (Bay Alarm Medical survey); adult children are more than
  twice as likely as seniors themselves to plan the purchase (TheSeniorList.com, 2026). This is a
  legitimate, citable structural feature of this market, not just an assumption.
- **Elder-fraud market urgency is real and strongly sourced:** FBI IC3 reports $7.7B lost by
  adults 60+ in 2025, up 59% year-over-year. This is a strong, real, defensible market-urgency
  statistic for the deck.

## 6. iHEARtest — B2B licensing and funnel-value benchmarks
- **Mimi Hearing Technologies raised ~$31-32M total** (no disclosed valuation or revenue) and has
  real scale proof: 4 million partner devices across 80+ headphone models. **No per-device or
  per-integration fee has ever been publicly disclosed** — this is the single biggest data gap in
  this space; treat Mimi as a scale/growth proof point, not a pricing benchmark.
- **A defensible B2B licensing structure**, drawn from comparable health-tech "diagnostic engine
  as a service" deals: a modest annual platform/integration fee ($25K-$100K) plus a small
  per-device or per-active-user royalty, or a 5-10% revenue share if bundled into an OEM
  partner's hardware.
- **No direct "free screening app to hardware purchase" conversion benchmark exists anywhere.**
  The best available bounding proxies: audiology-industry paid-lead value ($45-90 per lead) and
  disclosed hearing-aid patient LTV (~$5,200) — both from paid-clinic funnels, not free-app
  funnels, and should be labeled as bounding proxies, not confirmed analogs.
- **Separately worth noting:** the SonicCloud/Sonitum "Johns Hopkins co-branded" partnership
  claim (used in earlier competitor research, not about OTCHealth's own products) could not be
  substantiated in any public source — worth a grain of salt if this comparison is used anywhere.
