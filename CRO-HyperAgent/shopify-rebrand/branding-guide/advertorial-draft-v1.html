A Family Story

Marvin Posey, my mother Kim's father, in the 1950s — house calls, hearing aid case in hand.

He came to your living room. We ship to your door.

By Matt Moore · OTCHealth

My grandfather didn't have an office. He had a car, a case, and a list of houses to visit. That was hearing care in 1947 — you called, and someone real showed up.

My parents, Mark and Kim, kept that going for decades, running their own hearing practice the same way: in person, unhurried, honest about what would actually help and what wouldn't. I grew up watching them do it.

What's changed since then isn't the need. It's the markup. Somewhere between my grandfather driving to your house and today, the same basic help got wrapped in a $2,000 to $5,000 price tag, an appointment you have to schedule weeks out, and a sales pitch you didn't ask for.

The moments I built this for

Maybe none of this is new to you. It wasn't new to me either, until I started paying attention to how often it comes up:

  • — Asking someone to repeat themselves at a loud dinner table, more than once.
  • — Turning the TV up loud enough that everyone else leaves the room.
  • — Missing half of what's said in a group conversation and just nodding along.
  • — Not hearing the doorbell, or the phone, from the next room.

None of that means something is wrong with you. It usually just means everyday moments have gotten a little quieter than they used to be, and most people wait years before doing anything about it — usually because the fix seems like a bigger deal than the problem.

Three generations, one idea: real hearing help shouldn't require a clinic appointment or a five-figure price tag.
