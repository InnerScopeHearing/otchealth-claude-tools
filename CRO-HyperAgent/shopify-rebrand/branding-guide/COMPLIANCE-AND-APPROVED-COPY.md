# Compliance Rules + Approved Copy — READ BEFORE PRODUCING ANYTHING
### These are hard constraints, not style suggestions. OTCHealth's whole regulatory position depends on getting this right.

## The core rule

**iHEAR TReO is a Personal Sound Amplification Product (PSAP), NOT a hearing aid.** It may ONLY be marketed as sound amplification for general wellness/everyday situations. It must NEVER claim to treat, diagnose, cure, or mitigate hearing loss. The FDA's PSAP exemption from full medical-device regulation depends entirely on labeling and promotional materials NOT establishing an intended use tied to hearing impairment. Real precedent: FTC v. Global Concepts Limited (2018) — FTC settled against a PSAP maker for claiming the product "can improve people's hearing," almost exactly the risk OTCHealth carries. This rule applies to EVERY surface — hero headlines, trust badges, FAQ answers, comparison-table claims, image captions, and quiz-result copy — not just paid ads.

**The future iHEAR Matrix line (a real, FDA-authorized OTC hearing aid) is a completely different regulatory category and is NOT customer-facing yet.** Do not produce any assets implying TReO is a hearing aid, and do not use FDA-clearance/registration language anywhere in TReO materials.

## Hard no's for every asset Folio produces

1. **No AI-generated on-camera "people," anywhere, brand or performance creative.** This is not a style preference — it's a live legal/enforcement risk. The FTC's 2024 rule bans AI-generated fake reviews/testimonials (penalties up to $53,088/violation, active enforcement as of Jan 2026). New York's synthetic-performer disclosure law takes effect June 2026. There is a documented, ongoing FDA/media enforcement pattern against AI-influencer health-product scams. Every "person" in every asset must be a real, consented customer or real staff member.
2. **No "before" imagery outside the advertorial.** Never show a person cupping their ear, looking frustrated, or otherwise dramatizing hearing loss as a deficit in brand/hero/PDP imagery. The advertorial page is the one narrow exception where a "problem moment" is allowed (see ASSET-SHOT-LIST.md section g) — but even there, never imply treatment of an existing medical condition.
3. **No audio-toggle "hear the difference" before/after demo.** No competitor in this category does this, and it's a compliance trap — it visually/audibly implies a treatment effect a PSAP cannot legally claim.
4. **No health-outcome stat claims tied to visuals** (e.g., dementia-risk statistics, "X% improved hearing"). This is the single most legally exposed pattern in the competitor set (Audien does this — do not follow).
5. **No red anywhere in comparison/pricing graphics, no countdown timers, no starburst "SAVE" tags, no sweepstakes overlays.** This is the MDHearing/Audien "infomercial" register — explicitly the thing OTCHealth is trying not to look like.
6. **No isolated "FDA APPROVED"-style seal/badge for TReO, ever.** TReO is not FDA-approved or cleared — it's exempt as a PSAP. Any FDA-related language is reserved for the future Matrix line only, and needs direct legal sign-off before it's ever produced.
7. **Every real customer testimonial used in imagery/copy must avoid implying impairment-was-treated language** (e.g., a quote like "I can hear my grandkids again after being nearly deaf" is a live liability — reframe around convenience/amplification, not restoration).
