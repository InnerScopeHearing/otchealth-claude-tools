# Compliance Rules + Approved Copy — READ BEFORE PRODUCING ANYTHING
### These are hard constraints, not style suggestions. OTCHealth's whole regulatory position depends on getting this right. Updated 2026-07-13 to align with Folio's Vol. 02 Complete Brand Book (the newer, stricter, authoritative wording rule — supersedes this file's earlier approved-hook examples).

## The core rule

**iHEAR TReO legally IS a Personal Sound Amplification Product (PSAP) — that's the regulatory category that exempts it from full medical-device rules.** But as of Folio's Vol. 02 brand book (2026-07-12), the wording rule is now STRICTER than "PSAP-safe framing": **never say "PSAP" or "sound amplifier" anywhere forward-facing — not in headlines, not in body copy, not in ads, not in disclosures.** This is a voice/brand decision on top of the legal one: TReO must never claim to treat, diagnose, cure, or mitigate hearing loss (the legal floor), AND separately, the product category names themselves ("PSAP," "sound amplifier," "sound amplification product") are now banned from all forward-facing copy as a brand-voice rule. Use situational/benefit language instead — see the "Do" list below. This rule applies to EVERY surface — hero headlines, trust badges, FAQ answers, comparison-table claims, image captions, and quiz-result copy — not just paid ads.

**The future iHEAR Matrix line (a real, FDA-authorized OTC hearing aid) is a completely different regulatory category and is NOT customer-facing yet.** Do not produce any assets implying TReO is a hearing aid, and do not use FDA-clearance/registration language anywhere in TReO materials. Never call Matrix "FDA approved" either — it's in the FDA OTC class, a different, more precise claim.

## Voice rules (Folio Vol. 02 — Do / Don't)

**Do:**
- Name specific listening situations: a loud table, the TV, a group conversation, a doorbell
- Compare to real prices (e.g. "$4,000 clinic vs. $99" / see PRODUCT-AND-CONTACT-FACTS.md for the full verified comparison figures)
- Lead with the heritage story where it fits ("three generations since 1947" — see the heritage-date framing rule in PRODUCT-AND-CONTACT-FACTS.md)
- Keep "not a hearing aid" genuinely legible near any efficacy-adjacent copy
- Be direct: "Shop TReO," "Take the free hearing check" — plain verbs, no euphemism

**Don't (all hard rules, not style preferences):**
- Say "PSAP" or "sound amplifier" anywhere forward-facing
- Say "treats," "cures," "fixes," or "mitigates" hearing loss
- Say "hearing aid" in reference to TReO specifically
- Use red, countdown timers, starburst badges, or sweepstakes overlays — anywhere
- Bury the disclosure — small is fine, hidden is not

## Hard no's for every asset produced

1. **No AI-generated on-camera "people" attached to a fabricated name, quote, or "verified buyer" badge.** AI-generated imagery IS approved for illustrative/atmospheric/lifestyle content (Matt's explicit call, accepting that risk as owner) — the FTC/NY-law risk this file originally flagged is specifically about fake testimonials/reviews with a fabricated identity, not AI imagery itself. Real, named people (Mark, Kim, Matt, real customers) always use real photos, never a generated stand-in.
2. **No "before" imagery outside the advertorial.** Never show a person cupping their ear, looking frustrated, or otherwise dramatizing hearing loss as a deficit in brand/hero/PDP imagery. The advertorial page is the one narrow exception where a "problem moment" is allowed — but even there, never imply treatment of an existing medical condition.
3. **No audio-toggle "hear the difference" before/after demo.** No competitor in this category does this, and it's a compliance trap — it visually/audibly implies a treatment effect a PSAP cannot legally claim.
4. **No health-outcome stat claims tied to visuals** (e.g., dementia-risk statistics, "X% improved hearing"). This is the single most legally exposed pattern in the competitor set (Audien does this — do not follow).
5. **No red anywhere, no countdown timers, no starburst "SAVE" tags, no sweepstakes overlays.** This is the MDHearing/Audien "infomercial" register — explicitly the thing OTCHealth is trying not to look like.
6. **No isolated "FDA APPROVED"-style seal/badge for TReO, ever.** TReO is not FDA-approved or cleared — it's exempt as a PSAP (internally; never say "PSAP" externally per the voice rule above). Any FDA-related language is reserved for the future Matrix line only ("FDA OTC class," never "FDA approved"), and needs direct legal sign-off before it's ever produced.
7. **Every real customer testimonial used in imagery/copy must avoid implying impairment-was-treated language** (e.g., a quote like "I can hear my grandkids again after being nearly deaf" is a live liability — reframe around convenience, not restoration).
8. **Never say "PSAP" or "sound amplifier" in any forward-facing surface** (see core rule above) — this is new/stricter than this file's earlier version and supersedes it.

## Standing disclosure language (locked, matches Folio's brand book exactly — use this verbatim)

"TReO is not a hearing aid. It does not diagnose, treat, cure, or mitigate hearing loss. If you suspect hearing loss, consult a hearing professional."

(Note: this replaces this file's earlier disclosure draft, which used the now-banned phrase "sound amplification product" — the version above is Folio's actual locked text, used verbatim in the brand book's disclosure-tag and type specimen.)

## The OTC ligature as a typographic device (new, from Folio's Vol. 02 brand book)

The OTC icon mark can stand in for the literal word "OTC" inline in headlines/category labels/promo lines (e.g. "Save 50% on [OTC-icon] Hearing Aids"). Use sparingly — once or twice per page, never in body paragraphs. It replaces only the word "OTC," never "OTChealth," "health," or any other word.
