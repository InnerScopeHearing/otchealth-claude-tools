# OTCHealth / iHEAR TReO — Brand Strategy Brief
### Source: Shopify Living Doc Sections 8 & 10 (CRO research, 2026-07-10). This is the design-direction ground truth for the full branding guide + hero section build.

## The one strategic call that matters most

**Do NOT clone the Hims/Ro/AG1 minimal-hero aesthetic.** Those brands can run a six-word hero and one moody photo because $15-60M/month in upstream paid media already taught the market what the product does before anyone lands on the page. OTCHealth has neither that brand familiarity nor that ad budget, and it's selling into a category most consumers don't understand yet (most people don't know what changed with OTC hearing aids in 2022, or the difference between a PSAP and a hearing aid). A minimal, mysterious hero will strand visitors before they understand what they're buying. Real-world validation: multiple Ro/Felix/Bioverse/Eden clones have become visually indistinguishable from Hims by copying the aesthetic without the ad engine behind it — "you copy the endpoint of a funnel and attach it to the beginning of a journey. It doesn't convert. It confuses."

**The right synthesis:** keep the *premium visual grammar* premium DTC health brands use (warm neutral canvas, real-people photography, editorial type, exactly ONE brand accent color used sparingly, generous whitespace) but pair it with a more **explanatory, educational** treatment — a short qualifying/orientation element near the top (mild-to-moderate framing, "is this right for you"), visible credibility signals (Mark/Kim's real dispenser credentials), and a compressed "how OTC hearing aids work" visual explainer above the fold. Borrow Jabra Enhance's "test-first" credibility pattern and Apple's "simpler than you think" framing, wrapped in Hims-grade visual polish rather than Hims-grade minimalism.

## Visual specifics

- **Color**: abandon clinical blue/white entirely. Warm neutral canvas (bone/cream, not stark white) + dark ink text + exactly one accent color used as a signal, not decoration. **No red anywhere** — red on comparison/pricing modules signals urgency/loss and is the single biggest "infomercial" tell (see MDHearing/Audien below). Use a single muted warm tone (terracotta, deep amber) at low saturation for any "recommended" highlight.
- **Typography**: pair a warm editorial serif/display face for headlines with a clean grotesque for body/UI — signals "human" and "precise" simultaneously. Consider a monospace treatment for fine-print/regulatory disclosures (reads as "data/label," reinforcing legitimacy rather than looking like buried legal text).
- **Photography**: real customers and Mark/Kim (credentialed, shown with real dispenser credentials — Licensed Hearing Aid Dispensers, NEVER "audiologist") over stock; warm/natural light (~2700-3200K); reject "product on white" clinical shots as a primary treatment; **zero AI-generated humans anywhere, brand or performance creative** — this is a hard rule, not a preference (see Compliance doc).
- **Avoid the "template-y" tells**: unmodified Dawn-theme defaults, app-injected UI clutter, incoherent photography mixing styles, weak color discipline, badge-stacking (see below).

## Competitor positioning (direct OTC hearing-aid storefronts — study these, don't copy)

Researched: Eargo, Lexie (Bose-powered), Jabra Enhance, MDHearing, Audien, Audicus, Sony CRE-C10/E10.

**Most premium-feeling**: Jabra Enhance and Audicus — structured comparison-table pricing (multiple models, clear feature deltas), foreground licensed-audiologist credentials, real financing math (APR/monthly numbers shown, not just "financing available") — reads as a considered medical purchase, not a gadget upsell. Eargo's premium feel comes from restraint: sells a design/founder story before revealing price (a luxury-brand pattern).

**Most budget/infomercial-feeling (avoid this register entirely)**: MDHearing (celebrity testimonial, aggressive "$5,000 clinic" price-anchoring, red strikethrough badges stacked with countdown urgency) and Audien (low absolute prices + "no hearing test needed," red-accented CTAs, sweepstakes overlay, percentage-stat walls). BBB has flagged MDHearing for a falsely-claimed A+ rating; persistent complaints about hidden return-fee friction behind "risk free" framing.

**Universal trust mechanisms — surface ALL of these above the fold or within one scroll:**
1. Risk-free trial period (45-100 days) with money-back guarantee — the single most repeated signal across every competitor.
2. Financing with real monthly-payment numbers shown inline near price (Affirm/Klarna "As low as $X/mo" pattern), not just "financing available."
3. Free online hearing test/self-assessment as the primary top-of-funnel CTA (not "buy now").
4. Ongoing remote/telehealth support from real people, positioned as included.
5. Explicit warranty terms (2-3 years).
6. FDA-registered/cleared-category language distinguishing from unregulated PSAPs — **CAUTION: this applies to the future iHEAR Matrix OTC hearing-aid line ONLY. TReO is a PSAP and must never use FDA-clearance language.** See Compliance doc.

## Two-track creative model (why this matters for Folio specifically)

There are TWO different photography/video jobs here, not one:
- **Track A — Folio's job**: evergreen, premium, brand-asset photography/video. Quarterly cadence. Used on-site, in email, and as the "hero tier" of paid creative. This is what this brief and the asset shot list are for.
- **Track B — NOT Folio's job**: high-volume, fast, disposable UGC-style ad variants (a separate performance-marketing pipeline, 4-7/week starting cadence). Folio's photography becomes the *reference* for Track B's "brand asset kit" (approved phrases, past winners, sounds-like/doesn't-sound-like guide) but Folio should not be asked to produce that volume — using premium studio photography as raw ad creative is a documented mistake (reads as "closing," not "interrupting," and underperforms on cold traffic).

## "How OTC hearing aids work" — the compliance-safe format

Zero competitors in this category use an animated explainer or an audio-toggle "hear the difference" before/after demo — this is a deliberate compliance choice, not a missed opportunity (implies a treatment effect a PSAP legally cannot claim). The proven, safe format: **a well-labeled static cutaway diagram** (Eargo's approach — labeled "Figure 1/3" style, showing device placement relative to the ear, in the brand's warm palette, not a clinical blue/white register) **paired with a 45-90sec narrated video** (Mark or Kim narrating) that teaches the viewer how to read the diagram. Do not build an audio-demo. Do not use health-outcome stat claims tied to visuals (Audien's dementia-risk stat bar is the pattern to avoid — most legally exposed pattern in the category).

## Comparison/pricing graphic — how to avoid the infomercial trap

One clean anchor statement beats a crossed-out price + percentage badge + countdown stacked together. Outcome-framed copy over raw discount framing: "**same core amplification technology, without the $2,000+ audiologist office markup**" (tested +27% lift in fintech pricing-page research) rather than a bare "SAVE $2,900" badge. Clean 2-3 column table (Traditional Hearing Aid / CVS OTC Devices / iHEAR TReO), rows: price → what's included → trial period → warranty → where you buy — model on Warby Parker's plain-prose-plus-table pattern. No starbursts, no countdown timers, no sweepstakes overlays, generous whitespace (whitespace itself is a credibility signal — dense badge-stacked layouts read as "hiding something").

## Trust badge system

3 badges per row max (guarantee + shipping + warranty grouped — the Casper/Hatch pattern), placed directly adjacent to the ATC/checkout CTA (highest-leverage placement, not just the footer). Simple line icons, not photographic seals. Financing as inline "As low as $X/mo" text, not a standalone badge. **FDA-related language for the future Matrix line needs direct legal review before production — no isolated "FDA APPROVED" seal-style badge; precise inline text phrasing only, and not for TReO.**
